EMI Calculator

e.g. 10,00,000
Annual rate quoted by the lender

Results are illustrative estimates based on the assumptions stated on this page. They are not financial, tax or legal advice. Actual figures from banks, fund houses or the tax authorities can differ.

Enter the loan amount, yearly interest rate and tenure to see your monthly EMI (equated monthly instalment), the total interest you will pay and how much of each year's payments goes to principal versus interest.

How to use the EMI Calculator

  1. Type the loan amount in rupees. Commas are fine.
  2. Enter the annual interest rate offered by your lender.
  3. Enter the tenure and choose years or months.
  4. Press Calculate. Change any value afterwards and the result updates automatically.

Formula and how it works

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)

P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly instalments. If the rate is 0%, EMI is simply P ÷ n. Total interest is EMI × n − P.

Assumptions: interest is charged on the reducing balance, EMIs are paid at the end of each month, and no processing fees, insurance or prepayments are included. Banks usually round the EMI to whole rupees, so their schedule can differ by a few rupees.

Example

A loan of ₹10,00,000 at 9% per year for 5 years has a monthly EMI of about ₹20,758.36. Over 60 months you repay ₹12,45,501.31 in total, of which ₹2,45,501.31 is interest.

Frequently asked questions

How is EMI calculated?

EMI uses the reducing-balance formula EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1). Each payment first covers the interest accrued that month and the rest reduces the principal.

Does a longer tenure reduce my EMI?

Yes, a longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI, but you pay more total interest because the balance stays outstanding for longer.

Why does my bank's EMI differ slightly?

Lenders may round EMIs, charge processing fees or insurance, or use a different day-count convention. This calculator shows the plain mathematical EMI.

Is my data stored or sent anywhere?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser and the numbers you enter are never uploaded.
