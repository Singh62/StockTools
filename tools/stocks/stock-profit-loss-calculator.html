Stock Profit and Loss Calculator

Flat amount, charged on buy and on sell
STT, exchange, GST, stamp duty etc. Check your contract note

For education and arithmetic only. Not investment advice or a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Trading and investing involve risk of loss; charges, taxes and market conditions will change real outcomes.

Enter your buy price, sell price and quantity. Add brokerage and other charges to see what you actually keep after costs, and the price you must sell at just to break even.

How to use the Stock Profit & Loss Calculator

  1. Enter buy price, sell price and quantity.
  2. Enter your broker's brokerage per order (₹0 for most delivery trades with discount brokers).
  3. Enter an estimate of the other charges as a percentage of each side's value. Your contract note shows the exact figure.
  4. Press Calculate to see net P&L, return and the break-even sell price.

Formula and how it works

Gross P&L = (Sell − Buy) × Qty
Charges = 2 × Brokerage + (Buy value + Sell value) × Charges%
Net P&L = Gross P&L − Charges
Break-even sell price = (Buy × Qty × (1 + c) + 2 × Brokerage) ÷ (Qty × (1 − c))

c is the charges rate as a decimal. Real statutory charges (STT, exchange fees, GST, SEBI fees, stamp duty) differ between delivery, intraday and F&O and between buy and sell, and they change. This calculator therefore uses one blended percentage that you enter instead of hard-coding rates that may be out of date.

Example

Buy 100 shares at ₹100 and sell at ₹110, with ₹20 brokerage per order and 0.1% other charges: gross profit ₹1,000, charges ₹61, net profit ₹939 (9.39% of ₹10,000). The break-even sell price is about ₹100.60.

Frequently asked questions

What charges should I enter?

Take them from your broker's brokerage calculator or a recent contract note, expressed as a percentage of turnover per side.

Does it include capital gains tax?

No. Tax on profits depends on holding period and your income and is not included.

Does it work for short selling?

It models buy-then-sell trades. For a short sale, swap the buy and sell prices and check the charges.

Is this investment advice?

No. It is arithmetic on the numbers you enter.
