CAGR tells you the steady yearly growth rate that would turn your starting value into your ending value. It makes investments held for different periods comparable.

How to use the CAGR Calculator

  1. Enter the value at the start and the value at the end.
  2. Enter the holding period in years (use decimals for months).
  3. Press Calculate to see CAGR and total return.

Formula and how it works

CAGR = (Ending ÷ Starting)^(1 ÷ Years) − 1

CAGR assumes the money was invested once at the start and nothing was added or withdrawn. For regular investments such as SIPs, the right measure is XIRR, which this tool does not compute. Dividends are not included unless you add them to the ending value.

Example

₹1,00,000 growing to ₹2,00,000 in 5 years is an absolute return of 100% and a CAGR of 14.87% per year.

Frequently asked questions

CAGR or absolute return?

Absolute return ignores time. CAGR converts it into a yearly rate so a 5-year and a 2-year investment can be compared fairly.

Can CAGR be negative?

Yes, if the ending value is lower than the start.

Is CAGR the same as XIRR?

No. CAGR is for a single lump-sum; XIRR handles multiple cash flows on different dates.

Does a high past CAGR predict the future?

No. Past returns do not guarantee future results.
